How Property Investment Makes You Money

By Rockwell FinancialOctober 5, 2026

Ask most people what makes a good investment property and they will point to one thing: will it grow in value?

Growth matters. But it is only half the picture.

Property investment in Australia generates two types of returns at the same time, and understanding both changes how you evaluate any property you are looking at.

The two returns in property investment: capital growth and rental income

The first return is capital growth. This is the property’s value rising over time. You do not see this money week to week. It builds in the background over years and is realised when you sell, or when you use the increased equity to make your next move.

The second return is rental income. This is the rent the property earns along the way. This is the income stream you receive while you hold the property. It helps cover the ongoing costs of owning it – the loan repayments, council rates, insurance, property management fees and maintenance.

Both returns matter. They just do different jobs at different times.

How rental income works in practice

Rental income is what keeps a property financially manageable while you hold it over the long term. The more the rent covers the holding costs, the less the property costs you out of your own pocket each month.

When the rent exceeds the holding costs, the property is positively geared, i.e. it puts money in your pocket.

When the holding costs exceed the rent, the property is negatively geared, i.e. it costs you to hold, but may offer tax benefits and long-term growth potential.

Understanding your rental income relative to your holding costs is one of the most practical questions to ask before buying any investment property.

How capital growth works over a long hold period

Capital growth is where the most significant financial gains in property tend to accumulate, but only if you hold long enough for the growth to compound.

Here is a simple illustration:

A property purchased for $600,000 that grows at an average of 6% per year would be worth approximately $1,074,000 after ten years. That is $474,000 in capital growth. That growth was built not by anything you did, but simply by holding a well-located property in a market with strong long-term demand.

CoreLogic data supports this. Properties that resold at a gross profit had a typical ownership period of 9.1 years for houses. Properties that sold at a loss had typically been held for just 6.1 years. Time in the market is the variable that separates the two outcomes.

How the two returns work together

The real power of property investment comes from both returns working at the same time. The rental income helps you hold the property without it becoming a financial burden. The capital growth builds value in the background over years. Together, they create a picture that neither return delivers on its own.

Different properties lean different ways. Some produce stronger rental income with more modest growth. Others offer stronger long-term growth potential with lower rental yields. Neither leaning is automatically better. It depends on what a family is working toward and what they can comfortably carry month to month.

This is why evaluating a property on one number alone, whether that is the yield or the suburb’s recent price growth, gives you an incomplete picture. Both returns matter, and both need to work for your family’s situation.

Understanding both returns before you buy
