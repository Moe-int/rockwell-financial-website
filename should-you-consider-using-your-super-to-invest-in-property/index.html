Should you consider using your super to invest in property?

By Rockwell FinancialJuly 8, 2025
Your superannuation is likely your second-biggest asset after your home — and for many Australians, it’s underutilised. Through a self-managed super fund (SMSF), it’s possible to invest in residential property and take greater control of your retirement strategy.

Self-Managed Super Funds (SMSFs) offer a personalised approach to retirement savings. By managing your own super fund, you can tailor your investment strategy to align with your specific financial goals and risk tolerance, potentially leading to higher returns over the long term.

But is it right for you?

SMSFs offer unique benefits, including concessional tax rates and the ability to own tangible assets like property.

However, they also come with complexity: strict compliance rules, limited borrowing options, and ongoing administration responsibilities.

SMSFs responsibilities:

  • Compliance: Adhering to regulatory requirements is essential.
  • Investment Decisions: You are responsible for all investment choices, which requires a solid understanding of markets.

Through financial planning partners you can get expert guidance on if a property through SMSF is right for you, support in SMSF establishment, investment strategy, and ongoing management to ensure your fund operates effectively and in compliance with regulations.

An SMSF strategy may suit experienced investors with a clear long-term vision, strong cash flow, and a desire for more control. At Rockwell, we support our clients through selecting the right property opportunity — ensuring any decision supports their broader financial goals. We work with your independent financial partners to ensure all parts of your property investment strategy are tailored to your goals.

Key Takeaway: With the right support, SMSFs can be a powerful tool for retirement planning.

Disclaimer: This article is for general information purposes only and does not constitute financial, investment, or tax advice. SMSFs are complex and not suitable for everyone. You should seek professional advice tailored to your individual circumstances before making any financial or investment decisions.
