What Is Rentvesting and Why Are More Australians Doing It?

By Rockwell FinancialJune 22, 2026

It takes over a decade to save a 20% deposit in most Australian capital cities. For a growing number of families, that maths simply does not work. So they are doing something different. Rentvesting is a property investment strategy in Australia that is reshaping how everyday families think about getting into the market.

What is rentvesting and why is it growing in Australia?

Rentvesting means renting where you want to live and buying an investment property where the numbers make sense. You maintain your lifestyle and your location while your investment property has the potential to build equity and generate rental income somewhere else.

The logic is straightforward. A family renting in a suburb they love might need $200,000 or more for a deposit to buy there. An investment property in a well-located growth area might require significantly less.

Rentvesting allows families to enter the property market years earlier and start building equity while continuing to live where they want.

What does the data say about rentvesting as a property investment strategy in Australia?

The data shows this is more than a niche strategy.

According to ABS data, 8,283 first-home buyers took out investment property loans in 2024, a 12% increase on 2023. Rentvesting loans grew at 21.4% annually, more than double the growth of traditional owner-occupier first-home buyer loans.

Westpac’s 2025 Home Ownership Report found that 54% of first-home buyers are now considering rentvesting as their primary strategy to enter the market.

That is a significant shift in how Australian families are approaching property ownership.

Is rentvesting the right approach for your family?

Rentvesting is not a one-size-fits-all approach. It depends on borrowing capacity, income stability, lifestyle preferences, and long-term goals.

Some families will be better suited to buying their own home first. Others may find rentvesting better suits their long-term goals and puts them in a stronger financial position over time.

The right answer depends entirely on your individual circumstances, which is exactly why understanding your options clearly before making a decision matters so much.
