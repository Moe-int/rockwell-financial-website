Thinking About Retirement in Your 40s or 50s? Here Is What to Consider.

By Rockwell FinancialSeptember 7, 2026

Here is what most Australians in their 40s and 50s have in common: no real idea whether they can afford to retire.

Not a rough sense. No idea at all.

They are thinking about retirement without having ever sat down and checked whether the picture in their head matches the numbers behind it.

Why so many Australians in their 40s and 50s have not checked

It is not because they do not care. It is because life is full. Work, kids, mortgages, school fees.

Retirement feels like a problem for future-you. It is specific enough to worry about, but distant enough to defer.

And unlike a bill that arrives in the letterbox, the retirement gap does not send a reminder. It stays quiet until someone looks. For most people, that moment comes later than it should.

What thinking about retirement actually looks like at 45 or 55

Thinking about retirement in your 40s or 50s does not mean planning your last day of work. It means understanding three things:

  1. What you have now.
  2. What your current savings and assets are likely to generate if nothing changes.
  3. And whether the gap between those two things is something you have time to address.

That third question is where most families get their first real surprise. Not because the situation is necessarily dire, but because they have never looked at it clearly before.

What people discover when they actually check

When people finally sit down and look at the numbers, two things tend to happen. Some find they are closer to where they want to be than they assumed. Others find there is a gap that needs addressing. Both outcomes are more useful than not knowing.

Finding out early gives you options. If you are 45 and you find a shortfall, you have fifteen or twenty years to respond. You can make small, considered adjustments that compound over time.

If you find the same shortfall at 60, the options are fewer and the pressure is greater.

A small adjustment made early requires far less than a large correction made late. Time is the variable that matters most, and it is the one thing you cannot get back.

How property investment could fit into your thinking about retirement

Property investment is one of the tools Australian families use when thinking ahead.

A well-located investment property held over the long term can open up options, like more flexibility around when you choose to work, assets that generate rental income, or a faster path to paying down debt.

It is not the right fit for everyone, but for families who have the borrowing capacity and a long enough horizon, it is worth understanding how it works.
